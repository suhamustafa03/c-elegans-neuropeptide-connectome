What the archive actually contains

91 files total: 80 real recordings used in this analysis, plus 11 "scrambled-control" files in a separate subfolder, with filenames duplicating 11 of the 80 real recordings (presumably a shuffled/surrogate set for the original lab's own use). The scrambled files are kept fully separate here and are not used in this analysis — their exact intended role was not independently verified, so they are not treated as a negative control.

Each real recording is a single .h5 file: per-neuron GCaMP calcium traces (gcamp/trace_array, timepoints × neurons, 82–163 neurons depending on the recording) plus time-aligned behavior channels (velocity, angular velocity, head angle, body-angle variants, pumping, worm curvature, reversal state and events). Behavior channels are pre-aligned to the neural sampling rate in every recording checked — confirmed directly, not assumed.

Why this cannot be a connectome-linked question

Ruled out, not attempted

Neuron indices in these files are anonymous per-recording numbers (0–118, etc.) — there is no verified mapping from an index to a named neuron identity in this archive's main files. A separate label-mapping file (dict_neuropal_label.jld2, CC BY 3.0 US, same record) was downloaded and MD5-verified, but its contents use Julia's JLD2 object-serialization format: reading it with h5py raised TypeError: Unknown reference type on every type-defining attribute, so its structure could not be decoded without installing Julia's own JLD2.jl — explicitly ruled out rather than attempted. No neuron-identity claim is made anywhere in this analysis, and all neural features are population-level (recording-wide summary statistics and per-recording PCA components), never tied to a specific neuron index.

Marker-free vs. marker-present, not "spontaneous" vs. "stimulated"

37 of the 80 recordings contain a stim_begin_confocal timestamp; 43 do not. The source paper describes 30 heat-stimulus datasets and a separate "animals without any stimulus" comparison group — corroborating, but not exactly matching, this archive's 37/43 split (the counts differ and no source checked explains why). Recordings are therefore labeled "marker-free" and "marker-present" throughout this site, never "spontaneous" or "stimulated" — that stronger claim isn't established by anything inspected.

Relationship to the original paper's own analysis

Atanas & Kim et al. (2023, Cell, Fig. 2D) already train linear decoders predicting velocity at various time offsets from current neural activity, reporting that past velocity (up to ~20s back) could be decoded but future velocity could not. See Interpretation for exactly how this project's protocol differs from and extends that specific analysis.
