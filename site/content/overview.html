Research question

Within individual whole-brain calcium-imaging recordings of freely behaving C. elegans, does current and recent whole-brain neural population activity improve out-of-sample, time-blocked forecasting of future locomotor velocity and reversal onset, beyond a behavior-history-only baseline?

Why this question, and why it changed

This project originally asked a different question: whether an inferred neuropeptide connectome improves prediction of measured neuron-to-neuron signal propagation (Randi et al. 2023 data). That data's reuse terms turned out to be unresolved, and rather than use it without a clear license or wait indefinitely on an author reply, the project searched for a license-clear substitute and found one: Atanas, Kim et al.'s (2023, Cell) whole-brain activity dataset, explicitly CC BY 3.0 US licensed. That dataset cannot support a connectome-linked propagation question — it has no stimulation-response structure and no usable neuron-identity mapping (see Data) — so the question itself had to change, not just the data source.

The resulting question is deliberately narrower and already has a close precedent: Atanas & Kim et al. (2023, Fig. 2D) report that linear decoders could predict past velocity from current neural activity but could not predict future velocity. This project is a leakage-aware replication and extension of that specific result — not a claim of new general discovery about brain–behavior links. See Interpretation for exactly how it extends that prior work.

Scope and honesty up front

  • This is an observational, associational comparison. A feature's predictive contribution is evidence of statistical association, never proof that it causes the outcome.
  • This analysis does not test signal propagation or connectome structure of any kind. The earlier neuropeptide-connectome direction is paused, not completed — see Status.
  • Neuron indices in this activity data are anonymous per-recording numbers, not verified identities — no claim here links a specific named neuron to a result.
  • Recordings, not animals, are the reporting unit throughout; animal identity is not established from the source files.
