Results

Verified, exact results for velocity and reversal-onset forecasting, both evaluation schemes.

Headline, by task

Velocity forecasting: a null result. No model consistently beats the behavior-history-only baseline. Reversal-onset forecasting: a real but behavior-dominated signal. Neural activity alone clears its own recording's prevalence baseline, but behavior history is the stronger predictor and combining the two adds little on average. Full numeric output: results/behavior_forecast_summary.txt and results/behavior_forecast_paired_comparisons.txt (per-recording paired comparisons, produced by src/behavior_forecast/paired_comparison.py, unit-tested) in the repository.

Reversal-onset — paired, per-recording comparisons

Comparing medians across the group (above) can hide whether an effect holds within each recording. Checking per recording:

  • Neural-only's PR-AUC exceeded that same recording's own prevalence rate in every recording where the comparison was defined — 42/42 (marker-free) and 37/37 (marker-present), in both the blocked and forward-chaining schemes. This is the basis for calling the neural signal real and above-baseline.
  • Combined did not consistently beat behavior-only, and the exact win count is not stable across reruns. Combined's PR-AUC exceeded behavior-only's in roughly half of recordings across all four conditions (two independent runs of the identical pipeline on the identical data gave 24/42, 21/42, 28/37, 21/37 the first time and 22/42, 22/42, 18/37, 18/37 the second — see results/behavior_forecast_paired_comparisons.txt for both), with a median per-recording difference of only about ±0.01 PR-AUC in either direction in both runs. The win count moving between reruns is itself informative: it confirms the effect is too small and close to zero to be a reliable difference, not an artifact of one run. This is the basis for saying combining the two feature blocks adds no reliable value, not that neural activity's contribution is "independent" in a statistically tested sense — no formal significance test was run on this difference.

PR-AUC medians shown. The same qualitative pattern holds in every condition: neural-only clears the prevalence/persistence baseline; behavior-only is stronger; combined is close to behavior-only, sometimes marginally above and sometimes marginally below it.

The persistence baseline's ROC-AUC is consistently below 0.5 (0.269–0.274 across all four conditions). This is not an error: the onset label excludes frames already reversing at time t, so being in a reversed state at t is, by construction, anti-correlated with a new onset in the next window. The prevalence rate, not persistence, is the correct reference point for PR-AUC here.
